## 1. Executive decision

The pre-Week-1 program has two historical surfaces, followed by one outcome
access:

1. Complete the already-frozen four-law T230 suite on the canonical 54-slate
   Foundry v12 panel, together with its frozen support switch and incumbent
   comparators.
2. Run the eight-cell historical factorial specified here:
   incumbent-native versus frozen all-boom population, five versus twenty
   candidate-origin books, and complete-union line-194 versus the frozen
   support-switched T230 retrieval law.
3. On the same matrices, run all four frozen raw T230 laws, the scenario-ticket
   law, the existing comparator catalog, one fixed convex-excess expected-max
   law, and one 230-native distinct-block-supported ladder. Expensive
   population generation and cross-scoring may not be repeated for selectors.
4. Run a deterministic exact/bounded maximum-coverage oracle diagnostic and
   add two generation experiments that existing historical inputs support: an
   actual hard-230 generate/filter/replenish preset and one non-Atlas,
   equal-visit, game-regime-stratified tail-discovery schedule.
5. Freeze exact 4-, 14-, and 80-entry final books for every intended selection
   law and every feasible generated population.
6. Only then perform one controlled realized-outcome grade covering the entire
   predeclared catalog.

The experiment does **not** reopen Atlas. The minimal frozen Atlas test lost
`-1.53` mean candidate ceiling with `19/21/14` better/worse/tied and carries a
permanent-close disposition. No Atlas F1/F2, mixed Atlas dose, high-ceiling
world-ranker repeat, or top-nine proxy relabeling belongs in this matrix.

The unresolved interaction is different. The exact frozen all-boom law raised
mean raw five-seed pool ceiling by `+9.06` (`187.58 -> 196.64`) and roughly
doubled the deduplicated union (`550 -> 1,106`), while the inherited path moved
selected-book maximum only `+1.34`, a null. That inherited path compressed the
union back to roughly one native seed book before line-194 selection. The open
question is whether tail-native complete-union retrieval can convert the
already-demonstrated supply.

## 4. Generate once, score once, select many times

The factorial must not multiply its expensive work by eight.

```text
one immutable slate/source snapshot
             |
             +--> generate P0 for R0..R19 once
             |
             +--> generate PB for R0..R19 once
                            |
                 canonical global roster union
                 with every population/origin occurrence
                            |
          cross-score every unique roster once on R0..R4
                            |
       P0/PB x K5/K20 x fold/final lineage masks
                            |
       R194, four raw T230 laws, support switch,
       scenario tickets, and frozen comparators
                            |
                 exact rank-80 + prefixes 4/14/80
```

Per slate, persist one canonical roster table and one score matrix:

- roster identity is the sorted nine-player canonical ID tuple;
- lineup ID is a deterministic hash of roster identity and slate identity;
- every occurrence retains population, origin seed, generator-family tags,
  generation law, role injection, and source identities;
- the global union contains every unique roster in either `P0` or `PB` across
  all twenty origins;
- the matrix has one row per global-union roster and exactly five ordered
  10,000-world ordinary-R blocks; and
- population, `K`, fold, and final-fit candidate sets are masks over these
  rows, never separately recomputed matrices.

Required hashes bind the ordered lineup IDs, occurrence/provenance table,
player-row identity, each world block, full matrix, and every derived mask.
Selectors may consume only those frozen objects.

Peak memory is controlled by processing one slate at a time and writing the
matrix once. Population and selector cells must not remain resident together
when a streaming slice suffices. The k=20 generation/reconstruction and the
all-boom unique-fill optimizer calls dominate runtime; the selector catalog is
cheap relative to rebuilding candidates.

## 5. Candidate-origin cross-fit law

Let candidate-origin labels be `R0..R19`, while evaluation blocks are exactly
`R0..R4`. For held-out fold `h in {R0,...,R4}`:

1. `K5` begins with occurrences from origins `R0..R4`; `K20` begins with
   occurrences from `R0..R19`.
2. Remove the held-out origin `Rh` from the provenance visible to selection.
3. A roster remains eligible if and only if it has at least one occurrence in
   that cell's population and K-origin set after that removal.
4. Therefore a roster generated only by held-out `Rh` is excluded. A duplicate
   roster also generated by any training origin remains eligible, but the
   held-out occurrence, tags, counts, and attribution are stripped.
5. Origins `R5..R19` are independent candidate-discovery origins, not
   evaluation blocks. They remain eligible in every fold and never contribute
   selector-fit score columns.
6. Fit every selector using score columns from the four ordinary training
   blocks only.
7. Freeze the selected IDs before exposing that fold's held-out score columns
   to metric calculation.
8. Evaluate only on the held-out ordinary-R block. Candidate-generation worlds
   from `R5..R19`, realized outcomes, matchup outcomes, and other arms may not
   enter evaluation.

For the final fit, all candidate origins in the cell and all five ordinary-R
score blocks are available. The final fit is distinct from every fold fit and
is frozen for the later controlled realized grade.

Candidate eligibility is population-specific. A roster appearing in both P0
and PB does not transfer provenance from one population to the other. Likewise,
a PB `K20` occurrence cannot make the same roster eligible in PB `K5`.

## 6. Exact budgets and retrieval laws

Every selector produces one deterministic ranking to depth 80. Exact books are
the rank prefixes `[:4]`, `[:14]`, and `[:80]`. The runner must replay each
prefix independently from the retained marginal trace and require exact ID
equality. An insufficient eligible population is a failed cell, never a reason
to reduce a budget or borrow a lineup from another population.

### 6.1 Complete-union R194

Use inclusive `score >= 194` on the fit worlds. At each rank choose:

1. largest marginal count of newly covered fit worlds;
2. largest individual inclusive-194 event count;
3. largest fit-world mean score; and
4. ascending lineup ID.

When marginal gain reaches zero, continue with the same individual-count,
mean, and ID order until rank 80. No candidate prefilter or fixed candidate
quota precedes this selector.

### 6.2 Frozen support-switched T230

For a four-training-block fold, literal inclusive-230 coverage is used only
when:

- every training block has at least one opportunity world in which some
  eligible candidate reaches 230; and
- total inclusive-230 opportunity worlds across the four blocks are at least
  100.

For the all-block final fit, all five blocks must be nonzero and total
opportunity must be at least 125. Otherwise the already-frozen block-robust
finite ladder is used. The fallback rungs and incremental weights are exactly:

```text
>=210: 1
>=220: 2
>=230: 4
>=240: 8
>=250: 16
```

The support switch points to an already-materialized raw-law ranking. It does
not run a fifth selector or choose a law from observed performance.

## 8. Frozen scenario-ticket selector

### 8.1 Identity and purpose

**ID:** `support-switched-event-component-tickets-ge-230-v1`
**Role:** Tier-E mechanism comparison of explicit scenario partitioning versus
greedy union coverage
**Inputs:** the same eligible candidate IDs, fit score matrix, block labels,
and inclusive-230 event matrix used by `coverage-ge-230-v1`
**Forbidden inputs:** held-out scores, realized outcomes, player traits,
ownership, field data, learned embeddings, a caller-selected cluster count,
or any similarity threshold

The clustering deliberately has no `K`, distance cutoff, random seed, or
learned parameter. It partitions the sparse event graph exactly, so it cannot
be tuned after seeing which clustering resolution scores best.

### 8.2 Event graph and deterministic clusters

For one fit scope, let `E[i,w] = 1` exactly when eligible lineup `i` scores
`>=230` in fit world `w`.

1. Retain only opportunity worlds `T = {w : any_i E[i,w] = 1}` and candidates
   with at least one event in `T`.
2. Construct an undirected bipartite graph with one vertex for every retained
   world, one vertex for every retained candidate, and edge `(i,w)` exactly
   when `E[i,w]=1`.
3. Its connected components are the scenario clusters. This is the unique
   maximal partition in which no candidate can hit worlds in two different
   clusters. No Jaccard threshold or stochastic clustering is permitted.
4. World identity is `(block ordinal, zero-based world column)`. Candidate
   identity is the canonical lineup ID.
5. Component key is the SHA-256 of canonical JSON containing its sorted world
   IDs and sorted candidate IDs.

For component `c`, retain:

- `q_c`: number of opportunity worlds;
- `b_c`: number of distinct fit blocks represented;
- candidate count;
- block-by-world counts; and
- component key.

If the event graph forms one giant component, the result must report that the
scenario partition degenerates toward ordinary coverage. The method may not be
changed or reclustered in response.

### 8.3 Component allocation schedule

Build one component schedule to rank 80:

1. **Breadth sweep.** Sort components by descending `b_c`, descending `q_c`,
   descending candidate count, then ascending component key. Visit each active
   component once in that order. If there are more components than remaining
   book slots, the prefix stops; lower-ranked components receive no ticket.
2. **Proportional continuation.** After every initially active component has
   received one ticket, choose the next active component by the largest exact
   D'Hondt quotient `q_c / (a_c + 1)`, where `a_c` is tickets already assigned
   to that component. Compare quotients by integer cross multiplication, never
   floating tolerance. Ties use descending `b_c`, descending `q_c`, descending
   candidate count, then ascending component key.
3. A component becomes inactive when no unselected candidate in it covers a
   previously uncovered component world. It never receives a redundant ticket
   merely to satisfy its proportional share.

The breadth sweep supplies external scenario spread; D'Hondt supplies a fixed,
house-monotone allocation to larger surviving scenarios. This exact sequence
creates one prefix-stable rank rather than separately optimizing three book
sizes.

### 8.4 Ticket choice within a component

On a component visit, select its unselected candidate by:

1. largest marginal count of newly covered worlds within that component;
2. largest individual inclusive-230 count within the component;
3. largest individual inclusive-230 count across the complete fit scope;
4. largest fit-world mean score; and
5. ascending lineup ID.

Update that component's covered-world mask and `a_c`, then return to the global
allocation schedule. Because the connected components have disjoint candidate
sets, a lineup can never be assigned to two components.

### 8.5 Sparse-support and exact-book fallback

Use the same support gate as the frozen T230 policy: every fit block nonzero
and at least 100 opportunity worlds in a fold, or every block nonzero and at
least 125 in a final fit.

- If the gate fails, use the complete frozen
  `block-robust-bounded-tail-ge-210-250-v1` rank verbatim.
- If the gate passes but event components exhaust before rank 80, append the
  same block-robust rank after removing already-selected IDs. Preserve its
  relative order exactly.
- If fewer than 80 eligible candidates exist even after fallback, fail the
  scope. Do not relax 230, lower a budget, or draw from another cell.

The final scenario ranking is materialized once to 80; exact books are its
first 4, 14, and 80 unique IDs. Replay must reconstruct the component graph,
schedule, marginal choices, fallback boundary, and all three prefixes exactly.

### 8.6 Required scenario diagnostics

Retain component count, largest-component opportunity share, component block
breadth distribution, tickets per component, opportunity coverage by
component, fallback rank start, selected event-vector duplicates, pairwise
event Jaccard, and comparison with literal greedy coverage. These are
descriptive; no diagnostic changes the frozen method.

## 9. Simulated evaluation before the outcome read

For population `U`, exact-N book `B`, held-out world set `W`, and
`t in {194, 200, 210, 220, 230, 240, 250}`, compute:

```text
A_t(U) = mean over w in W of 1[max score among U in w >= t]
H_t(B) = mean over w in W of 1[max score among B in w >= t]
miss_t = A_t - H_t
conversion_t = H_t / A_t, when A_t > 0
conditional_regret_t = mean[(max_U - max_B) * 1[max_U >= t]]
```

Also retain:

- candidate and lineup-world event counts by threshold and block;
- worst-block hit rate, block range, training-to-heldout gap, and support-switch
  pass/fallback status;
- event-union divided by summed individual event rates;
- pairwise event intersection/Jaccard and duplicate event vectors;
- bounded event-matrix rank and full 4/14/80 final-book spectra only where
  already required;
- exact eligible, union, and selected counts;
- P0/PB roster overlap and origin multiplicity;
- generation family and origin attribution;
- realized-independent topology diagnostics such as game concentration,
  stack/bring-back shape, and internal coherence;
- lineup membership stability across folds; and
- runtime, peak memory, optimizer visits/calls, matrix shape, and hashes.

Five folds are averaged into one equal-weight vector per slate. Slates, not
worlds, candidates, or folds, are the inferential unit. Report paired per-slate
effects, season-stratified summaries, leave-one-slate and leave-one-season
influence, exact sign-flip inference where feasible, and fixed-seed Monte Carlo
otherwise. World-level intervals are forbidden.

The simulated layer may be inspected after every intended final book is
already immutable. It does not choose which books receive the realized grade;
the complete frozen catalog is graded once. This prevents a simulated-result
screen from becoming an undisclosed selector-selection step.

## 14. Final Week-1 interpretation rule

The aggressive candidate for prospective use is not automatically the cell
with the highest historical weekly maximum. Read the evidence in this order:

1. Did the population create more held-out ordinary-R 230 opportunity?
2. Did retrieval convert that opportunity at the same exact book size?
3. Did the effect transfer across slates and seasons rather than one fold or
   one week?
4. Did neighboring 220 and 240 behavior corroborate rather than contradict the
   fixed-230 result?
5. Did the single realized grade support the same direction without an
   influential-slate explanation?

A favorable answer may place a frozen strategy in the Week-1 shadow fleet.
The incumbent money book remains the incumbent unless and until a separately
predeclared prospective gate is met.
