# Review of post-forensic suggestions

Date: 2026-08-15 05:10 CDT  
Source: `reports/2026-08-15-post-forensic-suggestions.md`

## Bottom line

The proposed direction is useful, especially P-versus-pool characterization,
realistic recourse, overlap measurement, and fixed-budget generator
reallocation. The source document's central numerical premise cannot yet be
used, however: both the published P oracle and the published
perfect-information recourse ceiling were solved with QB+1 and no bring-back,
while production candidates require QB+2 and a bring-back.

The current selected-lineup baseline remains valid. The provisional quantities
are the 78.994-point P-C attribution, its first-failed-layer counts, the
3.583-point H-P attribution used to dismiss pool admission, the no-floor
oracle comparison, and the +42.62-point hindsight recourse ceiling. These are
being recomputed in the separately frozen exact-stack addendum. None may be
used to promote a historical arm.

## Disposition by suggestion

### 1. Characterize P versus the generated pool — accepted and implemented

The exact-stack addendum reports minimum player-swap distance, P-player
appearance counts, salary and position spend, games spanned, largest team
block, QB stack size, bring-backs, aggregate ownership, four stack-constraint
cells, corrected gaps, tails, and first-failed layers. It first requires exact
reproduction of the published loose P result, then solves QB+2/bring-back-1.

This is the right diagnostic. Its output remains outcome-viewed hypothesis
generation, not an arm scorecard. A corrected P necessarily obeys every
explicit production construction rule, so the rule cells size the definition
error; they do not prove that relaxing a rule would help prospectively.

### 2. Compute realistic recourse — accepted with a strict interpretation

The data claim is correct. A read-only inventory found all 54 distinct
checksum-addressed player-world artifacts in each of the five selected Phase S
treatment panels (270 slate/seed artifacts total), with no missing URI or
digest and one common code identity. The frozen multi-seed factorial already
passed checksum, player-universe, world reconstruction, and selector-parity
gates for those artifacts.

A historical run is therefore technically possible without buying or
recreating data. It must use one predeclared decision time, observed final
points only for games completed by that time, zero points for unstarted games,
and the frozen simulated remaining-world matrix for late players. It must use
the already frozen `prospective-recourse-policy-v1`, exact-80 entry book,
candidate set, alternative cap, tail ladder, tie breaks, and upload-legality
contract.

The output is a descriptive policy-sizing estimate, not unbiased expected ROI
and not historical promotion evidence. The opportunity register should retain
both numbers with distinct labels: corrected perfect-information upper bound
and historical executable-policy estimate. It should not silently replace one
with the other.

### 3. Construction/recourse overlap — accepted in two stages

The exact-stack addendum now compares corrected P distance from:

- the selected weekly-best roster;
- the incumbent source entry chosen by the hindsight recourse solve; and
- the corrected hindsight recourse final roster.

This answers the upper-bound overlap question under one legality contract.
After the realistic recourse run is frozen, the same distance and structural
comparison should be applied to its final book. Construction and recourse must
not be added as independent opportunity sizes until this comparison is known.

### 4. Constant-budget generator reallocation — accepted prospectively

This belongs near the top of the 2026 shadow queue, after the corrected
diagnostic. The exact arm must hold fixed:

- total realized unique candidates per slate, not merely requested solves;
- five candidate seeds and five 10,000-world blocks;
- exact-80 selector, tail line, ordering, and tie breaks;
- source snapshots and all non-generator model inputs; and
- a receipt of requested solves, failed/infeasible/duplicate attempts,
  accepted unique candidates, and compute time by generator.

The source document overstates one point: replacing a large `lev` tranche with
boom/dark/role/scenario solves is not automatically compute-neutral. It can be
candidate-budget-neutral while changing solve cost. The shadow must measure
both realized candidate count and compute rather than assume equality.

Existing archetype and latent-role shadows already perform fixed-budget
candidate replacement without changing money lineups. The direct
`lev`-reallocation law should be a separately named prospective treatment,
not an after-the-fact reinterpretation of either shadow.

### 5. Close pool admission and salary floor — defer the broad closure

The route-admission mechanism remains independently rejected: it admitted 565
players while recovering only 2 of 17 omitted winner slots. That result does
not depend on the loose P oracle.

The broader claim that all pool admission is dead depended on the provisional
H-P value and must wait for corrected H-P. Likewise, the salary-floor
conclusion must be restated from the corrected QB+2/bring-back no-floor solve.
The addendum includes both checks. If their qualitative results remain the
same, record them in the kill list with corrected numbers.

## Additional defect found during review

The perfect-information recourse solver inherited the same QB+1/no-bring-back
defaults as P. Its published +42.62 mean gain is therefore provisional. The
solver now accepts and independently audits the production construction
contract; the corrective addendum recomputes it and the P-distance connection.
This affects no selected lineup or production decision.
