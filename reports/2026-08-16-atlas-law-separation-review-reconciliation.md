## Standing law-divergence rule

Research does not have one universal simulation law. Each result must name its
measurement law. A result measured on an experimental law may establish a
mechanism within that law, but it needs an outcome-free transfer cell before
being described as relevant to the current money law. This is an explicit
cost: additional compute, delayed decisions, and the possibility that an
effect is law-dependent.

Convergence is not appropriate merely to avoid that cost. Phase S deliberately
tested an alternative dependence/allocation law. Silently treating it as
production would erase the very distinction under study. The defensible rule
is explicit law receipts plus transfer, not an assumption that all research
must share one law.

## Transfer interpretation

The original transfer protocol already contains both kinds of requirements,
but they are now labeled separately without changing any threshold:

1. **Mechanical validity:** exact source law and seeds; 15 clean acquisition
   executions; complete 270-cell grid; immutable object/code/image receipts;
   finite `(players, 10000)` worlds; complete point-in-time player catalog;
   deterministic ranking/ties; legal exact solves; proxy upper-bound
   invariant; and no outcome input.
2. **Effect transfer:** after mechanical validity, pass all three frozen
   Part-A conditions: positive aggregate mean delta, positive seed mean in at
   least three of five blocks, and non-worse aggregate q25.

A mechanical failure produces no scientific transfer result. A mechanically
valid Part-A failure means the Phase S ranking effect did not transfer as
specified; it is evidence of law dependence, not an implementation failure.
The prior, fixed before the money-law result, is that the effect may shrink or
disappear when the joint opportunity law changes.
