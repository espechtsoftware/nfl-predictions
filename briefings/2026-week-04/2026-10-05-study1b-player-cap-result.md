# Study 1b result: capping any one player's share of entries (2026-10-05)

Written for the operator. The question: Week 4 put one player in about half of our entries. If no player could be in
more than about 30% of entries, would we do better?

## Answer: not at a cost you accepted
- **The cap does what a cap should.** On 36 historical slates (2023–24), building the lineups with the cap in mind:
  - kept the most-used player at about 32% of entries, against 61% today;
  - roughly halved the damage done when that player busts.
- **But it lowers the average finish.** The lineups finished about 3.5 percentile points worse on average.
  - The test allowed at most 1.5 points (your choice).
  - Even the worst case consistent with the data, a 7-point drop, is far past that limit.
  - A 25% cap cost about 4.3 points, and a 35% cap about 2.4. None passes.
- **Reshuffling today's lineups across contests cannot deliver the cap.**
  - About 38% of entries could not be moved without breaking the cap or the contest-overlap rule.
  - The top player stayed near 59% of entries.
  - On your real Week 1–4 contests it barely moved exposure (93 → 89 entries in Week 4) and left the money
    exactly the same.

## Something worth testing next (a lead, not a finding)
- The capped books cashed somewhat more often in the test:
  - about 6–9% more cashes;
  - far fewer slates with no cash at all (13–18% of slates instead of 32%).
- Study 1's game cap showed the same pattern. Spreading the book out lowers the AVERAGE finish but crosses the cash
  lines more often, because the lineups are less tied to each other.
- For cash-line contests, crossing the line is what pays. So this is worth its own test.
- These were side measurements, with no statistical test across five versions, so they prove nothing yet.
- An earlier study (the winners study) found the opposite: concentration paid at the cash lines. The new test is built
  so it can come out either way. It is item 14 on the study list.

## What this means
- **No change to how lineups are built.** The cap is not offered.
- The single-player risk you raised is real, and we now know its price: cutting it in half costs about 3.5
  points of average finish.
- Whether that price buys more cashes is the next test.
