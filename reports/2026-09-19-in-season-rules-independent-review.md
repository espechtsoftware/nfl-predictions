# Independent review of the in-season rules

September 19, 2026. Reviewed production policy branch `production/in-season-rules-20260919` at `00e3d779`, the relay at lab `d9753ae`, production `AGENTS.md`/`CLAUDE.md`, the operating handoff's ground rules, lab `LAB_RULES.md`/`COORDINATION.md`, the September 1 objective-change notice, and the amended Route Share gate. The objective is better results **this season, as soon as possible**. This is a recommendation to both teams, not a claim that a new selector has been adopted.

The new in-season track fixes the most serious error: a historical validation protocol must not become a blanket ban on improving the current system. I support prompt correctness repairs, immediate shadow preparation, weekly evidence, and distinguishing reversible use from a permanent default. However, several proposed rules still delay useful decisions or substitute a weak numerical rule for judgment.

## Changes I recommend now

| Rule | Finding | Recommended replacement |
|---|---|---|
| C: win two consecutive weeks or three of the first four; S: win three of four | Signs discard the size of gains/losses and favor frequent small wins over rare large gains. In the idealized independent fair-sign case, at least three wins in four occurs with probability 5/16: this is not a strong statistical confirmation rule. Four-week waiting is also unnecessary when transferable evidence already exists. | Review cumulative paired effect, uncertainty, mechanism, and downside every week. Make a **reversible in-season trial** recommendable as soon as its specific evidence package is ready. State uncertainty and the unchanged control explicitly. A trial is a decision under uncertainty, not a declaration of a confirmed effect. Stronger evidence supports broader adoption. |
| Revert after two losing weeks | Two weeks without the rare tail we want need not invalidate a useful tail policy. Automatic switching would itself be an untested selection policy. | Immediately stop for integrity failures. For efficacy, preregister candidate-specific review dates and material-harm criteria tied to the chosen utility and accumulated evidence. Retain the control for comparison and rollback. Distinguish ordinary outcome variance from forecast or implementation failure. |
| C uses player CRPS/MAE to approve component or world weights | Player marginals can improve while shared-world dependence or selected extremes worsen. World weighting can change dependence with little marginal movement. The project already has this failure class. | Require final served-law and fixed-lineup/book diagnostics at the consumer being changed, including predeclared tail scores. Use CRPS for distributions; report point MAE separately (it favors a median, not a mean). A point projection alone cannot supply full-distribution CRPS. Do not infer lineup efficacy from a player-score pass. |
| Route Share reads after six and ten graded weeks | Starting Week 3 means the first use could be Week 9, then Week 13. The amendment also calls the primary a “200+ threshold,” while the original is lexicographic **240,230,220,210,200**. It is unclear whether the 12-week/2,500-row/40-event floors apply to interim reads; if they do, the six/ten-week reads cannot pass. | Keep the original final scientific read intact. Add a separately named **weekly in-season decision record** from the first complete paired week. Define its endpoint and available support explicitly; never silently waive original floors. Earlier bounded use can be considered under the general trial path, with the actual uncertainty. Fix the interim wording before implementation. |
| Every positive development result must enter next week's shadow | An unsupported or vacuous arm can consume the whole build window; a plausible new signal may have no historical panel at all. | Shadow any mechanically ready, supported candidate with a clear hypothesis and a frozen comparison, prioritized by expected information and potential gain. A positive reused-panel score is neither necessary nor sufficient. Name the owner, compute limit and earliest week. |
| R: “a repair is not a bet” and exact parity on every other row | A correct repair can materially change the entered book; operational and predictive consequences still exist. Bitwise equality is not always realistic for nondeterministic floating reductions, as this morning's attribution showed. | Prove the broken contract and intended correction, run unchanged leakage checks, show exact identities and declared numerical tolerances on unaffected values, quantify propagation through the final consumer, and preserve rollback. No new NFL outcome wait is necessary for correctness. “Mechanism explained” alone does not make a model hypothesis a repair. |
| E: no evidence, and no rollback | Contest ordering, dose, and late swap can change the effective selection policy and operational risk. A “one-line change” is not necessarily simple in consequence. | Keep the operator's authority, but require mechanical rehearsal, explicit affected entries and a restore path. Classify the underlying change by what it does; calling a new optimizer “entry-side” does not erase its model risk. No new universal historical gate. |
| Every component, prefix and metric must avoid any decline | On the Thursday pool even removing all prefix/block guards left zero passing swaps under the six component guards. This does **not** establish that equal-mixture utility fell or that sacrifices were large. Requiring universal dominance can make the search incapable of proposing a useful tradeoff. | Select one primary matching the user's objective; report the other quantities as explicit costs, with narrowly justified material-harm limits. Compare all model components, but neither an uncalibrated component nor every spreadsheet block gets an automatic veto. Freeze any new tradeoff experiment before its result. |
| Six seasons and fixed season-sign limits for permanent adoption | Historical coverage is valuable but some new licensed sources do not have six seasons. The September 1 L2 tail carve-out already says LOSO is reported rather than vetoing, which conflicts with the generic permanent-adoption wording. | Make validation proportional to the mechanism and available data. Preserve each old experiment's verdict, apply its own frozen rule, and carry L2 explicitly into the current hierarchy. Do not invent missing seasons or treat an evidence shortage as proof of no value. |
| “Selection is closed,” or a paid source “does not work” | Previous closures were broader than their tested implementations. Existing owner directives already require implementation, information, objective and budget scope. | Carry those four dimensions into every closure. New data, repaired inputs, different supported consumers, or a different objective can justify a new test. Repeated tuning of an unchanged failed arm remains exploration and must be labelled as such. |

The effect of the universal guards has already been measured in the [strict retrieval result](2026-09-19-fixed-k-retrieval-results.md) and [whole-book-only companion](2026-09-19-fixed-k-tradeoff-results.md). They justify measuring explicit tradeoffs; they do not show that relaxing a guard will improve real scores. The [direct-220 experiment](2026-09-19-direct-220-selection-results.md) also warns that optimizing a more attractive simulated number can fail on independent worlds.

## Rules that should remain, with practical clarification

Keep point-in-time data, unchanged leakage checks, legal complete books, explicit player/contest identity, walk-forward training, independent audit randomness, same-build controls when code changes, immutable artifacts, source ownership, launcher lanes and protection of running jobs. Keep archived original verdicts, development-look counts and independent reproduction. These protect the meaning of an improvement and prevent avoidable operational losses.

Separate **mechanics**, **exploration**, **confirmation**, and **the decision to use a candidate**. It is legitimate to inspect already-open development outcomes to understand failures and design a different model. It is not legitimate to call a resulting tuned comparison fresh confirmation. Bank991's separately held read is now explicitly authorized in lab `2bee35c`; freeze its amendment-5 reader and proceed. That earlier hold was never a reason to stop other work.

“Read immediately” means read a complete, validated result with the correct frozen reader, under its existing outcome-access authority. Missing or failed tasks cannot be silently dropped. Reader repairs and mechanical failures should be resolved promptly, not left as indefinite holds. Independent review should run alongside useful preparation; mechanical checks and reversible preparation do not each require another operator question.

Weekly monitoring and inference are different. Display all weekly results promptly. Fixed-sample confidence intervals do not automatically become valid stopping rules when checked every week; either label that record descriptive or implement a prespecified sequential procedure with its assumptions checked. Confidence-sequence methods provide such machinery, but do not create more independent NFL slates. [Howard et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.08240).

For forecasting, keep the marginal, lineup-total, and fixed-book-maximum views. Proper scoring after fixed transformations can target these distinct forecast properties; no single score diagnoses every failure. This supports extending the **existing accepted reader**, rather than creating a competing scoring implementation. [Pic et al.](https://ascmo.copernicus.org/articles/11/23/2025/index.html).

## Proposed governing wording

> The goal is useful improvements in the current season. Evidence requirements inform the strength and scope of a recommendation; elapsed weeks, an old experiment's endpoint, and a method-family closure do not independently prohibit a reversible trial. Each candidate states its mechanism, exact change, primary utility, evidence and known tradeoffs, earliest usable week, operational proof, unchanged comparison, monitoring plan, and rollback. The operator decides adoption. Scientific verdicts retain their frozen rules and are reported separately from that decision. Integrity failures always stop release.

For C/S candidates, replace the universal consecutive-win and consecutive-loss rules with the paragraph above plus a candidate-specific decision record. This does not require a new bureaucracy: one short record linked to the actual code, comparison and release proof suffices.
