## Decision in one paragraph

Production agrees with the review's central diagnosis: the next useful work is not another selector grid over
the same score matrix. Finish the already-running PREREG-060 screen, repair and run the already-built
PREREG-062 feature sweep, and run one cheap shock-equivalence audit. Those three reads decide the next and only
score-bearing branch: either one same-pool learned retrieval test (KG5, with incidence weighting as its single
controlled variant) or one small covariate-conditioned dependence test that can create different supply. Do not
build both branches before the routing evidence exists. Begin the `us_dfs` coverage/capture work in parallel
because it adds genuinely new pre-lock information. Keep the decision-diagram sampler to one bounded feasibility
spike, and use historical winners only as a locked retrieval challenge.

The purpose is to obtain an informative score comparison quickly, not to build a general research platform.

## The three-read routing packet

The next score-bearing experiment is chosen from exactly three development reads. Do not add a fourth selector
or feature search before making the branch decision.

### Read 1 — finish PREREG-060 unchanged

Let the current full process finish. Preserve the frozen D800 pool, bank-620 construction worlds, +900 held-out
worlds, K80, threshold ladder, and deterioration rule.

The report must answer only:

1. Does QD_NOV improve held-out winner utility or a frozen exceedance endpoint?
2. Does it preserve the 210/220 upper tail within the frozen 5% relative bound?
3. Are improvements present at useful K prefixes rather than appearing only at K80?
4. Which exact candidates, QB/game spines, and threshold-event worlds are rescued?
5. Is the result stable across seasons and not merely higher effective rank?

Do not revise QD_NOV after the read. Effective rank and independent-equivalent shots remain diagnostics.

### Read 2 — repair, smoke, and run PREREG-062

PREREG-062 is the correct first systematic feature-to-outcome screen and already covers much of the review's
paid-feature question. It replaces any proposal for a new generic feature sweep. Before its first association is
computed, make these narrow conformance repairs:

1. **Enforce one bank-620 cohort.** Accept exactly the sealed 088 bank-620 run, validate its bank and complete
   72-slate census, and reject additional runs. The current `nargs="+"` interface could repeat the same slate
   population across banks and create candidate-row pseudoreplication.
2. **Make missing columns explicit.** A missing frame column must produce an all-missing Series of the correct
   row length plus a coverage receipt, not a scalar `None` that can fail inside `dict(zip(...))`.
3. **Preserve legitimate zero values.** Replace truth-value expressions such as `value or np.nan`; zero
   ownership or salary-derived values must remain zero.
4. **Use the standard BH step-up decision.** Find the largest ordered p-value satisfying `p(k) <= q*k/m`, then
   reject every ordered hypothesis through `k`. The current independent per-rank boolean assignment is not the
   BH rejection set.
5. **Prove the frozen feature census.** Fail on extra post-hoc features, report per-feature/per-slate missingness,
   and publish F1/F2/F3 coverage before the associations.
6. **Make the family stratifier real.** Since `lev/boom` is declared a stratum rather than a feature, co-report
   within-family associations or a prespecified stratified aggregate. Do not let raw family mixture masquerade
   as a feature result.

Run one four-slate smoke after these repairs, then the full pass after PREREG-060 releases the single local-heavy
slot. The result only orders KG5 feature families. It does not promote a selector.

The report must include:

- full frozen hypothesis count and the exact BH cutoff;
- within-slate association, season-block uncertainty, and season signs;
- missingness/support by season and family;
- rescue relevance among unselected candidates;
- a clear `F1`, `F2`, `F3`, or `none` nomination.

### Read 3 — N2 shock-equivalence audit

Implement this next because it is inexpensive and determines whether the apparent leverage regret is learnable
or is repeated exposure to a few hindsight player shocks. It uses already-open development outcomes and has no
adoption authority.

Use the sealed 088 bank-620 D800 trace and exact roster-hash settlement. For each slate:

1. Identify every unselected candidate that beats the selected K80 maximum.
2. Pair it with the best selected comparator and compute the symmetric player difference.
3. Decompose the score difference into the frozen pre-lock center, atomic player residuals, a prespecified
   leave-season-out team/game residual, and an idiosyncratic remainder.
4. Without reselecting, clamp each player residual and then each game residual to its frozen center.
5. Build the omitted-candidate-to-shock bipartite graph.
6. Repeat the same summaries for boom, leverage, and matched random candidates using opportunity denominators.

Freeze the decomposition and thresholds before calculating results. A simple actionable branch rule is:

- **Distributed/learnable:** at least 12 slates across at least three seasons retain an omitted-over-book case
  after clamping the largest player residual on that slate, and at least half of the original case count remains.
- **Shock-concentrated:** fewer than one quarter of cases remain after that clamp, or one player/game component
  explains more than half of aggregate regret.
- **Ambiguous:** everything between those boundaries; permit a bounded KG5 screen but do not interpret a positive
  row-level result as independent evidence.

Required artifact: `selection_regret_shock_equivalence_v1.json`, self-hashed and bound to the exact trace and
settlement identities. Required report: concentration by player/game/slate, HHI/Gini, shocks needed for
50/80/90% of regret, leave-one-player/game persistence, seasons represented, and family denominators.

## One branch, selected from the routing packet

### Branch A — same-pool retrieval is justified

Take this branch only if PREREG-060 finds held-out complementary quality in D800 and the N2 audit is not
shock-concentrated. PREREG-062 selects the first feature family.

Freeze one KG5 efficacy experiment:

- exact same D800 candidates, admission, construction/audit banks, K80, and compute for all arms;
- control: D800_DEMAX;
- treatment: the minimum PREREG-061 hybrid of book-marginal value and one walk-forward phenotype estimate;
- first feature set: the single family nominated by PREREG-062; expand to the next nested family only under a
  preregistered continuation rule, never because individual historical features look attractive;
- no player IDs, slate IDs, winner labels, or candidate-family identity as predictive inputs;
- slate-total loss weight one; season-forward folds only;
- registered utility primary; raw weekly maximum and 194/200/210/220/230 landmarks co-reported.

#### Incidence weighting without an arm zoo

Build `candidate_incidence_receipt_v1.json` for every slate regardless of whether it becomes a treatment. Include
the exact roster/pool identity, rank and singular-spectrum summary of the candidate-by-player matrix, row leverage
distribution, source/family leverage mass, and selected/omitted mass.

If the outcome-blind weight-concentration gate passes, the one controlled KG5 comparison may include:

- equal within-slate row weighting; and
- the single frozen shrinkage weighting `w_i proportional to 0.5/n + 0.5*h_i/rank(A)`.

Do not search alpha. Do not add ordinary diversity, leverage quotas, or several model classes. Correlation-
preserving source controls are mechanism receipts, not additional portfolio arms. If a source family cannot beat
its control out of time, remove that family before the one score-bearing cohort.

### Branch B — belief/law or supply is justified

Take this branch if PREREG-060 finds the D800 pool barren or its critics cannot identify complementary candidates.
Do not spend the next cohort on KG5.

Build the intentionally small N4 upstream conditional-extremes test:

- unit: team-game roles, not candidate rows;
- roles: QB, two same-team receiving roles, and one opponent response role;
- covariates: participation/route concentration, total/spread, and one pace/pass-rate block;
- comparators: incumbent law, covariate-free extremal baseline, and one covariate-conditioned model;
- season-forward fit and evaluation;
- primary upstream endpoints: prespecified joint-exceedance Brier/log score, calibration by state, and one
  variogram/energy score;
- no lineup generation unless the conditional model wins upstream and materially disagrees on frozen candidates.

If it passes, run one equal-budget candidate generation arm and cross-score it under the incumbent and new law.
If it fails, close this law form; do not search state definitions or dependence families on the viewed panel.

### Branch C — stop

If PREREG-060 has no held-out improvement, PREREG-062 nominates no feature family, and N2 is shock-concentrated,
stop the historical selection branch. Preserve prospective 2026 candidate lineage and wait for new outcomes/new
information. A null routing packet is a useful result.

## Parallel work that does not delay the score branch

capture.

### N6 — matched historical-winner retrieval challenge

Build the harness now, but keep its first full read behind winner-registry v2. It must use one case per slate,
leave the complete slate out of fitting, insert the winner into a deterministic matched legal decoy set, hide the
winner identity, and report percentile, reciprocal rank, top-10, and top-50 retrieval. One immutable evaluation is
allowed per model identity.

The lab may exercise mechanics and run an explicitly incomplete unambiguous-winner subset, but must not label it
the 51-slate result or use disputed score/slate identities. This is model criticism, never historical promotion.

### N5 — one bounded legal-space sampler spike

Do not build a decision-diagram platform. Authorize one feasibility spike only after Branch A has a credible
critic or Branch B has an accepted law and a residual supply gap remains.

Hard scope:

1. tiny exact-enumeration parity fixture;
2. one reduced-slate legality/frequency test;
3. one representative full slate with a fixed memory/time cap;
4. oracle-by-CPU, unique roster/core count, and outside-critic frontier versus CBC;
5. automatic stop on state explosion or no meaningful support change.

No score-bearing panel is authorized by feasibility alone.

## Minimal acceptance and reporting contract

Every score-bearing routed comparison must report:

- mean and median weekly K80 maximum;
- changes at 194, 200, 210, 220, and 230;
- per-season and per-bank effects;
- K10/20/40/80 behavior;
- selected-book turnover and exact roster identity;
- pool oracle and regret as hindsight diagnostics, never promised recoverable gain;
- source/as-of/missingness receipts;
- exact candidate, selector, world-bank, source, image, and code identities.

The result is not a win if it merely increases entropy/effective shots, candidate-row AUC, or winner retrieval.
It must improve the frozen book utility/score endpoint without destroying the upper tail. Historical outcomes are
development evidence; prospective 2026 shadows retain adoption authority.

## Production review verdict

Adopt this plan with the sequencing above. The review's six proposals are not six equal experiments. N2 is the
cheap decision gate; PREREG-062 already implements the initial feature screen; N1 is a disciplined training
contrast inside the only justified KG5 path; N4 is the alternative branch when the pool is barren; N3 is parallel
new-information capture; N6 is model criticism; and N5 is a last, bounded supply feasibility test.

The first priority is to get from the current diagnostics to one realized-score comparison, not to finish every
possible subsystem.
