**Why the pass rate is low, in plain words.**
- **The rule is built to reject luck.** Pass-by-chance is about 1 in 3–4 per comparison; the fresh-draw check is the filter.
  Of the night's four picks, one held. That is roughly what chance plus one real small effect would give.
- **Most rules tested were shape rules, and shape is not where the points are lost.** Study 95 showed your mix is robust
  either way. The top finishers' shapes sit near the field's; the differences are in which players fill the slots
  (section 2).
- **The harness builds on the lab's own projections, your book on Fantasy Points'.** A rule that acts on the projection
  (shrink, value, fade, floors) is judged on different inputs than it would face live. The team already discloses this
  for study 106; it applies to 109 and to the value cap below. Your 10-06 rule ("six-season panels never gate; test on 2026
  full-data weeks") is the reason the W1–4 real-field replay should be read beside the harness for these.
- **Effect sizes (1–3 points) sit inside the bank-set noise (3–6 points).** A lever that is real but small cannot clear
  the rule in one read; only repeated reads or real weeks can.

## 2. The winners' patterns: the new screen

**Method.** Every Millionaire lineup of Weeks 1–4 (831k / 172k / 162k / 160k; 99% matched to the T-70 frame) and every
lineup in the 4444 / 555 / 333 / FFWC contests (14 contests, 12,219 lineups). Features from the T-70 frame (pre-lock):
salaries, positions, teams, games, kickoff times, our model's projection; "hot" from the actuals table (last game ≥ 2× the
mean of up to eight prior games across 2025–26, at least three prior games); the value tenth = projection per dollar ranked
within position among players projected 3+. Within-user Mantel–Haenszel odds ratios (users with 20+ Millionaire entries, or
3+ priority-contest entries) compare a user's lineups with the feature against his lineups without it, so the user's skill is
held fixed; by-contest ratios control the field. **Read the by-week columns, not the pooled ones:** Week 1's 823k lineups
dominate pooled figures, and Week 1 was the week value and punts hit. The ownership features of this run are wrong and
are not reported: the join took one roster-position row per player (ANY_VALUE over the position and FLEX rows of
`nfl_raw.contest_ownership`), which understates ownership; a re-run needs SUM(pct_drafted) per name (the laptop's review). The "Sunday book" column is the armed Week-5 construction rebuilt on Week 4's slate (the laptop's
`minprojcheck` check, LIVE + FAVHI), measured in Fantasy Points' projection terms.

## 3. The next tests, in detail

### 3.1 The recency fade (study 109): run it next, with a row-rule arm

- **What the fields say.** In Weeks 2–4 a lineup with a player coming off a 2× game was about half as likely to reach the
  top 1% as its own user's other lineups; the top 1% carried 0.4–0.8 such players against the field's 0.9–1.6; your contests'
  top 3 carried 0.47 against our 1.41. The pros' habit (selling last week's scorers; the 10-08 briefing) and the 2023–25
  history (hot players bust a little more and boom a little less; §7 there) point the same way. The crowd does the opposite
  (ownership rises with last week's points at z 14), so the fade is leverage where the crowd is.
- **On the Sunday book (W4):** 0.96 hot players per lineup; 20 of 26 lineups hold at least one (15 hold one, 5 hold two). A −2
  fade would move most of them; a one-per-lineup row rule would move the five.
- **Arms:** FADE2 (your −2 at 2×), FADE2_16, and **HOT1** (at most one hot player per lineup, through the `row_rule_sets`
  vehicle that carries te1 / low1 — the same mechanism study 91 used, so it has a known production path). A fourth, if the
  machine allows: the fade only on hot players Fantasy Points projects at 10%+ ownership (fade the chased ones, leave the
  cheap ones).
- **Definition:** the prereg's "last game ≥ 2× the mean of up to four prior games, at least two prior games, floor 5" is
  fine; the screen used eight prior games across seasons with a floor of 3, which reads the early weeks as well. Week 1 has no
  usable last game either way.
- **Prior from the ledgers:** study 65's recency arm (−1 at 1.6× plus a salary-rise flag, as an 8-row block *replacing* the
  cheap block) read −2.5; study 34's habit tilt no difference. Neither is this form; both ran on the older construction.
- **Order:** ahead of 106–108. The floor evidence is weaker and points the other way in your contests (section 4).
- **Production:** no fade switch exists. The term-block vehicle refuses negative terms (`own_bonus` keeps only positive
  values), so a fade needs either a one-line allowance for negative terms with tests, or the HOT1 row rule. The team has
  judged it Week 6; the row-rule form is the shortest path if it ever has to move faster.

### 3.2 A value cap: at most 4 of 8 skill players in the top value tenth

- **Rule.** For each skill player, value = FP projection per $1,000 ranked within position among players projected 3+ on the
  T-70 frame; a lineup may hold at most 4 skill players in the top tenth. Infeasible → re-solved without, recorded (as the row
  rules do). The cheap block, caps, row rules and shapes are unchanged.
- **Why.** The solver maximises the sum of projections, so it fills lineups with the players furthest above their price;
  the Sunday book averages 4.5 such players and 17 lineups carry 5–7 of 8. No winning group exceeds 3.9. The payoff flips by
  week (section 2.1): a cap keeps the bet at the winners' dose instead of making every lineup the same bet.
- **Against it, honestly.** The shrink (study 92) is the stronger version of this idea and read −4.4 on both draws in the
  harness, where "value" is the lab's own simulated value and does carry information on 2023–24 results. Expect the harness
  to lean against the cap too. That is exactly the case your 10-06 rule anticipated: read the W1–4 real-field replay beside
  it (Week 4 on FP, Weeks 1–3 on ours), and if the two disagree, a paper arm on Week 5's real results decides.
- **Cost on the Sunday book:** about 17 lineups change; the FP cost per lineup is the W4 real-book check's first output.
- **Production:** a `val4` row rule in `row_rule_sets`; the value tenth is a few lines in `union_reselect` on the frame after
  the FP override. Small, and the vehicle already carries the receipt and the fallback.

### 3.3 A salary-spread floor (stars and punts together)

- **What the fields say.** The spread of the 8 skill salaries is higher among the top 1% than the field in all four weeks
  (within-user odds 1.6 / 7.9 / 7.7 / 3.0); your contests' top 3 sit at $1,601 against the field's $1,448 and our $1,347.
  The mechanism matches the salary-tier finding of 10-07: our picks lose at $6,000–7,999, the tier a barbell empties.
- **On the Sunday book:** already $1,454 on average (the cheap block and the stars did this); a quarter of lineups sit under
  $1,280. A floor at about $1,400 would move those.
- **Rule (linear form):** every lineup holds at least one skill player at $7,800+ and at least one at $4,000 or under
  (the std is what was measured; the pair rule is what a solver can take). Infeasible → without, recorded.
- **Against it:** STAR1 (−5.8) and the price books read worse in the harness, and the harness reads the cheap block itself at a
  5–13% cost of expected big seats (studies 53, 100, 101) because 2022–24 were the weakest seasons for cheap players since
  2018. The 2026 fields say the opposite in every week. **Paper arm on real weeks** beside the cheap-block trial, which is
  being judged the same way.

### 3.4 A QB-rank cap (paper)

- **What the fields say.** The winners' QBs rank lower by projection than the field's in Weeks 1, 3 and 4 (9.6 / 13.8 / 9.5
  against 8.7 / 9.8 / 9.3); within user, a top-3-projected QB cut the odds of a top-1% finish in those weeks (0.37 / 0.69 /
  0.65) and raised them in Week 2 (3.12). Your contests' top 3 used QBs ranked 11.4; our entries 6.2; the Sunday book 6.6.
- **Rule:** at most 8 of 26 lineups on a QB ranked 1–3 by FP projection (the QB cap of 5 already bounds any one QB).
- **Prior:** QB cap 3 passed your rule once (study 100, +1.7) and failed the fresh draw (−0.4); the underdog / lower-half /
  cheaper QB rules were flat or worse (80, 82, 88). Weak; a paper arm.

### 3.5 Watch FAVHI's premise on Monday

The unconditional QB + own-RB pairing finished worse within user in three of four real weeks (0.62 / 1.04 / 0.22 / 0.66) and
in your contests in every week by contest (0.62 / 0.00 / 0.40 / 0.46); the top 1% carry fewer such pairs than the field.
FAVHI is the conditional form (expected winner of a high-total game) and passed twice in the harness, which is why it is
armed; the fields cannot read the conditional form on four weeks. Monday: the NORBMATE paper arm scores the book without it on
the same real fields. If FAVHI trails it in Weeks 5 and 6, that is the signal to revisit.

## 5. Not supported by the fields

- **A receiver-only bring-back.** The top 0.1% used an RB-only bring-back 45% of the time against the field's 26%; within user
  the receiver bring-back read 0.58 / 1.48 / 0.54 / 3.60. Study 71's "opponent's top receiver" result stands; do not add a
  receiver rule.
- **A late-window rule.** Week 1 punished late-window players (0.26), Week 2 rewarded them (5.0); it is the week's games.
- **A games-used floor.** The top finishers use fewer games than the field (5.4–5.7 vs 5.8–6.0), more than our book (5.15); no
  consistent direction within user.
- **A higher book projection.** Within user the higher-projected half of a user's lineups did better in Weeks 1 and 3 and worse
  in Weeks 2 and 4. Our lineups were the highest-projected in every field and scored below the field's average in Weeks 2–4.
