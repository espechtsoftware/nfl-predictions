# Was "about a 30% chance of a big win each week" ever true? The model's chances against what happened, Weeks 1–4

**For:** Erich. Your question (10-09): *"I thought any given week I had a 30% chance at a big win… Was that never true as I
was told?"*, and your request: *"I do want to do 3"*. This is step 3, a look back at what you entered. It changes nothing
for Week 5. Written by the laptop agent; the outside reviewer approved the method and reviewed the code.

## The short answer

- **The 25–30% figures were never measured on real contests.** They came from the test model on old seasons, against a
  simulated field that is easier than the real one: about 1 in 4 on the 10-05 sheet (study 24), about 30% in later test
  reads.
- **On your real entries against the real fields, the same model gave you about a 14% chance of a big win in a week.**
  Week by week: 7%, 21%, 8% and 19%.
- **Finding 1: zero big wins in four weeks does not show the model was wrong.** The model itself expected no big win in four
  weeks more often than not (55%).
- **Finding 2: where the model can be checked, it was too optimistic.** Week 1 was about right. In Weeks 2–4 your lineups did
  clearly worse than it predicted:
  - about half the cashes it expected;
  - about a quarter of the top-10% finishes;
  - one top-1% finish where it expected about a dozen.
- **Finding 2 means its 14% big-win chance was probably too high as well.** The shortfall grows with how high the finish
  is:
  - cashes came in at about half the expected rate (÷ 2.1);
  - top-10% finishes at about a quarter (÷ 3.9);
  - top-1% finishes far below (÷ 12.6), though that rests on a single finish, so it is noisy.

  A big win is a deep finish. Dividing the 14% by the cash and top-10% shortfalls gives about 6.5% to 3.5% a week: **about 1
  week in 15 to 1 in 30, possibly worse.** Four weeks cannot say exactly.

## What we compared

For each of your 534 entries in Weeks 1–4 (the same entries the money gate matched exactly to DraftKings' results):
- **What the model said before lock:** it played out each game 20,000 times with the scores the system used at T-70. Each
  time, your lineup and every real opponent's lineup in that contest got a score, and DraftKings' tie and prize rules were
  applied.
- **What happened:** your real finish, prizes and tickets.

**A big win** uses your 10-05 rule: $500+ in cash, or a ticket worth $300 or more.

Counts of your entries over Weeks 1–4. "Expected" is the model's average. "Likely range" is where its count landed in 90% of
its simulations. Your entries rise and fall together within a week, so these ranges are wide.

**A check that uses every entry:** for each entry, the model's chance that it would finish at or below where it really

3 and 4 they piled into the bottom (averages 33%, 33% and 31%). Your lineups kept finishing in the lower part of what the
model thought possible.

## Why Week 1 was right and Weeks 2–4 were not

The model's average score for your lineups and for your opponents' lineups, against what they really scored (DraftKings
points per lineup):

| Week | Your lineups: model → real | Opponents: model → real | Your edge, model | Your edge, real |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 130 → 145 | 128 → 143 | +1.8 | +2.3 |
| 2 | 134 → 105 | 129 → 118 | +4.8 | −12.9 |
| 3 | 131 → 122 | 128 → 133 | +2.9 | −11.7 |
| 4 | 141 → 116 | 129 → 119 | +12.0 | −3.7 |

- **Every week, the model rated your lineups above the average opponent's.** In Week 1 they really were, by about what it
  said. In Weeks 2–4 they scored 4–13 points below the average opponent.
- **Nothing in our records explains why Week 1 differed.** Three things changed, and none of them fits:
  - **The contests:** Week 1 was mostly the Millionaire and cash contests; Weeks 2–4 were mostly satellites. The gap shows
    in every contest type in Weeks 2–4, including Week 2's cash contests, so the contest mix does not explain it.
  - **How the book was built:** Weeks 1–3 all entered a Saturday-built book and Week 4 the Sunday T-70 book, so the build
    does not separate Week 1 from Weeks 2–3.
  - **Week 2's backup-quarterback error** (a backup projected as a starter on Saturday) is not in this: by the T-70 run he
    was projected at 0, and none of your Week-2 entries held him.
- **One known effect fits the pattern, but four weeks cannot prove it.** Choosing lineups by a model's own numbers tends to
  pick the players that model overrates most, so the chosen lineups fall short of their projections more than the field's
  do. Week 1 may simply have been a week where the games broke our way.

## What it means

- **The model overrated how high your lineups would finish,** from Week 2 on: its scores for your players were too hopeful
  relative to the field's.
- **This is not what inflated the 25–30%.** The test model scores its books on real results, so this optimism is already
  inside its numbers. What inflates them is its simulated field, easier than the real one, and a different contest mix.
- **Week 5 is different in one way:** the book is now built on Fantasy Points' projections, not our own. This check tests
  the old model, so it cannot say whether Week 5's book is better calibrated.

## My recommendation

- **Plan as if a big win comes about 1 week in 15 to 1 in 30, possibly worse**, not 1 in 3 or 4, and stake on that basis.
  How much is your call.
- **Run this same check every Monday from Week 5** on the new book, with the scores re-centred on Fantasy Points'
  projections (the ones the book is now built on), beside the paper comparison. Within a few weeks we would learn whether
  the new setup is more honest about its chances. It takes about 5 minutes.
- **Optionally, this week:** re-run Week 4 re-centred on Fantasy Points' Week-4 projections (we captured them). That would
  show whether the current setup would have been less optimistic on Week 4.

## Limits

- **Four weeks is a small sample.** The weeks, not the 534 entries, are the independent units. The Week 2–4 pattern is
  consistent, but it is three weeks.
- **About 1% of opponents' lineups couldn't be scored** (a player outside the model's player list, often a late scratch).
  They were left out, which makes the field look slightly stronger. That pushes the model's predictions down, so the
  optimism above, if anything, is understated.
- **Two contests were sampled:** the biggest contests used 100,000 opponents rather than every entry.
- **One hand entry is left out,** with no payout table captured, exactly as in the money gate.
